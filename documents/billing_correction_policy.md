# Synthetic Billing Correction & Corrected-Claim Review Policy (FICTIONAL)

> **This document is entirely synthetic and fictional.** It was authored for this prototype only.
> It is not a real Humana policy, is not derived from any real payer's guidelines, and must never
> be presented to a user as actual payer policy. Every service code, modifier, and identifier
> referenced elsewhere in this scenario is likewise fictional and does not correspond to a real
> CPT, HCPCS, or other industry code set.

## BILL-1: When a corrected-claim review is appropriate

A claim may be routed for corrected-claim review when a billing provider identifies that one or
more previously submitted billing fields (service code, modifier, units, or servicing-provider
identifier) do not match the documentation of the service actually performed. Routing for review
is appropriate only when the proposed corrections are supported by independently recorded service
documentation (e.g., an operative note, a units/anesthesia log, or a provider roster record) that
was not itself edited as part of the correction request. A proposed correction that conflicts with
independent documentation, or for which no independent documentation exists, is not ready for
corrected-claim review and should instead be returned to the submitter for clarification.

## BILL-2: Fields in scope for this review

This policy addresses exactly four billing fields commonly subject to correction: the service
code, the modifier, the billed units, and the servicing-provider identifier. Each field is
evaluated independently -- a supported correction to one field never implies that an unrelated
field is also correct, and an already-correct field must never be described as an error merely
because a correction was proposed elsewhere on the same claim.

## BILL-3: Distinguishing decision-identified from newly discovered discrepancies

The original recorded decision on a claim may identify only some of the discrepancies later found
during a corrected-claim investigation. A discrepancy first identified during investigation, but
not mentioned in the original decision, must be reported as a newly discovered discrepancy, not
attributed to the original decision. Both the decision-identified and newly discovered
discrepancies should be considered when assessing whether a corrected claim is appropriate.

## BILL-4: Analyst verification checklist before proceeding

Even when the available independent records fully support the proposed corrections, an analyst
handling corrected-claim review should still separately verify: (1) that the supporting records
were authored independently of the correction request and were not altered to match it; (2) that
the corrected servicing-provider identifier belongs to a provider who is appropriately credentialed
for the service and network at the time of service; (3) that any billed-amount change implied by a
corrected unit count is calculated correctly and is clearly labeled as a proposed, not an allowed
or paid, amount; and (4) that no other field on the claim was affected by the correction. This
review determines whether the claim is ready for corrected-claim submission -- it is not itself a
payment, coverage, or authorization decision.

## BILL-5: What this review does not do

A supported set of proposed corrections, and a recommendation to route for corrected-claim review,
never means: that a corrected claim has been submitted; that the original claim has been changed;
that a recorded denial has been reversed; or that payment has been approved or determined. Those
actions remain outside the scope of this review and require a separate, explicit downstream
process.
