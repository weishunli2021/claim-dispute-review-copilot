# Outpatient Imaging Policy (Synthetic)

> **Synthetic Data Notice:** This document is a fictional policy reference created for a prototype/demo. It does not represent an actual policy from any real health plan or payer, and contains no proprietary content of any kind. Plan names, network names, and identifiers are entirely fictional.

## IMG-1. Overview of Outpatient Imaging Services

Outpatient imaging services include X-rays, computed tomography (CT), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) performed outside of an inpatient hospital stay. Whether a specific imaging service is covered depends on the member's plan and the specific service code billed (for example, MRI-KNEE for a knee MRI). Associates should always confirm plan-specific coverage using the member's benefit record rather than assuming a service is covered based on this policy alone, since coverage rules can differ between Meridian plans.

## IMG-2. Outpatient MRI Prior Authorization Requirement

Outpatient MRI services, including knee MRI (service code MRI-KNEE), typically require prior authorization before the service is performed. Prior authorization is a separate approval process from the underlying benefit determination: a service can be a covered benefit and still require prior authorization before it will be paid. See the Prior Authorization Policy for how authorization requests are submitted, reviewed, and time-limited by an effective and expiration date.

## IMG-3. In-Network Imaging Facilities

Members generally receive the highest benefit level when outpatient imaging is performed at an in-network facility. Imaging performed at an out-of-network facility may be denied outright or paid at a reduced benefit, depending on the member's plan type. See the Provider Network Policy for how a facility's network status is determined and how it should be verified before assuming a claim outcome.

## IMG-4. Ordering Physician Documentation

An outpatient MRI must be ordered by a licensed physician, and the ordering physician is recorded on the claim separately from the servicing facility that actually performs the scan. Documentation of medical necessity from the ordering physician supports both the prior authorization request submitted before the service and, if the claim is later denied, a member appeal.
