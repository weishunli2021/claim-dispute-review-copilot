## APL-1. Member Appeal Rights Overview

A member who disagrees with a claim denial has the right to file an appeal requesting that the plan reconsider its determination. An appeal is a formal request for review and is separate from simply resubmitting a claim or requesting a new prior authorization.

## APL-2. How to File an Appeal

A member or their authorized representative may file an appeal in writing within the timeframe stated in the member's plan documents. The appeal should reference the claim in question and may include supporting documentation, such as a letter of medical necessity from the ordering physician.

## APL-3. Expedited Appeals

An expedited appeal may be available when a delay in decision-making could seriously jeopardize the member's health. Expedited appeals are reviewed on a shorter timeline than standard appeals.

## APL-4. What Happens After an Appeal Is Filed

Once an appeal is filed, the plan reviews the original determination along with any new documentation submitted. The outcome of an appeal may uphold the original denial, reverse it, or partially modify it.

## APL-5. Choosing Between an Appeal and Additional Documentation

Not every denial requires a formal appeal. For an AUTH_REQUIRED denial in particular, it may be faster to first determine whether a prior authorization request exists but was not yet approved, or to submit a new request with an effective window covering the date of service, before advising a member to file a formal appeal. A formal appeal is generally most appropriate when the plan's underlying determination itself is being disputed, rather than when missing paperwork can be resolved directly.
